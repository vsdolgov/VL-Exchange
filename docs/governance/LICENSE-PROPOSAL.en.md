[Apache-2.0](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0) covers copyright, patent provisions and trademark exclusions. Its patent grant is limited to contributor claims implicated by contributions; it does not clear third-party rights and is not a universal promise for every specification implementation. The [CC-BY-4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode) excludes patent and trademark rights. The [CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/) recommends dedicated software licenses for code; a software license rather than CC is therefore proposed for executable examples and schemas. This proposal has not been adopted.

Before adopting licenses, record the legal rights holder, authorization for work products and contribution scope, and review every copied file's provenance. Do not infer legal ownership from authorship. Agree a royalty-free/non-discriminatory commitment for essential patent claims and disclosure procedure for known claims. This is a separate decision that cannot be replaced by “open standard” wording. No blanket patent clearance has been established.

DCO 1.1 and Signed-off-by are proposed for contributions after license adoption; DCO certifies contribution provenance and does not transfer exclusive rights. Agree a CLA separately if needed; do not require transfer of commercial solutions beforehand. [DCO](https://developercertificate.org/).

## Portability of commercial rights

The initiative's central proposition includes retaining already-paid analytics. To deliver it, a publisher's terms must permit moving to a compatible platform without repurchasing the analytics, with agreed adaptation/support fees, usage scope and reissuance procedure. A format and conformance test cannot automatically impose this on authors.

Proposed standard terms: the customer retains execution rights within an agreed scope; changing a compatible host does not terminate them; the old installation entitlement is replaced; concurrent use remains contract-limited. For offline installations agree a migration window, deactivation and trusted procedure in advance because immediate revocation is unavailable. These provisions are neither an approved contract nor an implemented settlement service.
